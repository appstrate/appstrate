# RBAC invitation UX research

Research date: 2026-09-06. Scope: organization users, space members and pending invitations. This note separates documented product behavior from recommendations for Appstrate; it does not introduce a new authorization contract.

## Primary-source observations

| Product | Documented behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Relevant distinction                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Notion  | Workspace members are invited from People/Members. Guests are invited from a particular page, with an access level. Workspace policy can restrict who may invite guests.                                                                                | A contextual guest invitation combines identity and resource access, while organization administration remains distinct. [Notion documentation](https://www.notion.com/help/add-members-admins-guests-and-groups)                                                                  |
| Slack   | Guest accounts access specified channels. Owners/admins invite guests; existing guests can be added to further channels under separate rules. The directory can show their accessible channels.                                                         | Account type and channel membership are separate concepts. [Slack guest roles](https://slack.com/help/articles/202518103-Understand-guest-roles-in-Slack)                                                                                                                          |
| Slack   | Invited people can be added to channels before accepting. Their account becomes active upon acceptance and first sign-in. Owners/admins manage pending invitations centrally.                                                                           | Pending describes lifecycle, not a permission role. [Invited members](https://slack.com/help/articles/360024686174-Invited-members-in-Slack), [invitation management](https://slack.com/help/articles/360060363633-Manage-pending-invitations-and-invite-links-for-your-workspace) |
| Figma   | Members and guests can be invited to teams, folders or files, at or below the inviter's permission level. Guests normally reach explicitly shared resources; organization admins also have a central invitation path under the documented guest policy. | Resource-level delegation has a permission ceiling. Figma's member/guest distinction also depends on verified email domains, which is not Appstrate's model. [Figma documentation](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040453113-Add-members-or-guests-to-an-organization) |

These sources support separating identity, access and lifecycle. They do not establish that Appstrate needs domain-based classification, per-seat billing rules, guest approval requests, or Slack's pre-acceptance messaging behavior.

## Appstrate model and present gap

| Axis              | Existing meaning                                                                         | UI wording                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Organization role | `member`, `admin`, `guest`; `owner` is a separate, non-assignable ownership role         | Standard user, administrator, guest; always qualify the organization scope   |
| Space role        | Preset or custom permission bundle, plus implicit access rules                           | Role in this space; distinguish an explicit assignment from automatic access |
| Lifecycle         | An invitation is pending until accepted; membership and grants are created on acceptance | Pending invitation versus active access; a guest can be in either state      |

The current schema already stores one invitation's organization role and a list of `spaceAssignments`. Acceptance claims the token and applies membership/assignments in a transaction. Active space membership is a different object. [Invitation acceptance](../../apps/api/src/routes/invitations.ts), [assignment service](../../apps/api/src/lib/space-role-assignment.ts).

However, `createInvitation` first cancels all pending invitations for the normalized organization/email pair, then creates a new token containing only the incoming assignments. Therefore inviting the same person to A and then B before acceptance loses A and invalidates the first link. These writes are currently separate, so a create failure can also leave the original cancelled; concurrent creates need explicit serialization or uniqueness protection. This matters immediately when invitation creation is exposed in each space. [Invitation service](../../apps/api/src/services/invitations.ts).

## Recommended minimal contract

1. Keep one organization invitation lifecycle for a person, visible centrally and in each space listed by its assignments. A space view must derive pending rows from that same object; do not create an additional space invitation or a fake active member.
2. Keep two clear actions in space membership: add an existing organization user, or invite a new person as a guest. Show the fixed organization guest role and the chosen space role before submission. Organization administration remains the place to invite standard users/admins and manage all users, including guests.
3. Creating an external invitation still requires the existing organization `members:invite` authority. Space-only administration permits existing-user assignments subject to the current ceiling; it must not implicitly gain organization invitation authority. Any broader delegation needs an explicit backend policy decision, not a hidden UI fallback. [Organization route](../../apps/api/src/routes/organizations.ts), [space route](../../apps/api/src/routes/spaces.ts).
4. When the email already has a pending invitation, preserve its organization role and other space assignments. Adding a different space should extend that invitation atomically; an existing assignment for the same space should be displayed for explicit editing rather than silently overwritten. Preserve a valid token rather than accidentally invalidating links already shared. A conflicting organization-role request belongs in explicit central editing.
5. If an atomic extension is outside the current scope, return a clear conflict and direct the administrator to the existing invitation editor. A frontend read followed by whole-list PUT is not a concurrency-safe merge. Expired or cancelled invitations need explicit replacement semantics; an already-active user should use the existing membership path.
6. After submission, show the persisted pending row, intended role and copyable invitation link. Email dispatch depends on SMTP configuration, so success must mean invitation created, not email delivered. After acceptance, refresh both lists and show actual membership. Pending token access remains limited to the existing organization invitation authority.

## Implemented (2026-09-06)

- Recommendation 1: the space Members page renders the pending invitations whose assignments include the space from the organization's invitation list (`OrgInvitationsList` with `spaceId`), visible to holders of `members:invite` only.
- Recommendations 4–5, conflict variant: `createInvitation` no longer cancels-and-replaces. A valid pending invitation for the normalized (org, email) pair makes the create fail with 409 `invitation_already_pending` and `invitation_id`; the space form shows the conflict and links to the central editor. Concurrent creates are serialized by the partial unique index `uq_org_invitations_pending` (drizzle `0057`; `scripts/migration/0009` dedupes older duplicates if the index cannot be created). An expired-but-unswept pending row is cancelled so a fresh invitation can follow it.
- Recommendation 3 unchanged: the space form's external-invite option is shown only with the organization permission.

Atomic extension of a pending invitation from the space form (recommendation 4's first half) is not implemented: the administrator edits the invitation explicitly. The regression scenarios are covered by `apps/api/test/integration/routes/invitation-creation.test.ts` (A then B, concurrent, expired, cross-org) and `e2e/tests/spaces/delegated-members.ui.spec.ts` (pending row in the space, refused duplicate).

The recommendations below remain the reference for the rest.

These are recommendations, not claims about implemented behavior. The necessary regression scenarios are A then B before acceptance, concurrent A/B additions, repeated same-space submission, mixed-case email, an existing pending standard/admin invitation, expired invitations, acceptance concurrent with editing, and denial for a space administrator without organization invitation rights. Assert persisted assignments and token validity, not just a success toast.
